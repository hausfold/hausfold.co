You know the dance: drag a file, realise the window you wanted is buried under
four others, let go, dig it out, drag again.

**perch** ends it. Start dragging **anything**, flick up to the notch, and a
small shelf drops down to catch it. Pile in as much as you like, from as many
places as you like. Then grab any tile and carry the whole group to its real
home in one motion.

It has no Dock icon and asks for no permissions. It only ever sees what you
hand it: a drop, a right-click, `perch add`, or a folder you pointed it at.

These pages are the manual, and there is no shorter version of them.
Installing is one download, or one command:

```sh
brew install --cask hausfold/tap/perch
```

[Install](/docs/perch/install) is the whole path in order — the one macOS
setting that steals the drop if you leave it on, the signed download, pairing
the free iPhone companion, and the Nix flake. It is also what
[perch.hausfold.co](https://perch.hausfold.co) points at, which is the short
form to hand someone. The source and the
changelog are at [github.com/hausfold/perch](https://github.com/hausfold/perch),
and the menu bar's **Report a Bug…** opens the issue form there with your
version, macOS build, Mac model and install route already filled in: Homebrew,
haus, Nix, a dragged copy, or *install not recognised*, which is the line you
would never have thought to include and is often the answer. Nothing is sent by
that click: it opens a page and you write the report. There is no
telemetry in perch, so that form is the only way anything reaches us.

## What it holds

Files, folders, Photos exports, images dragged out of Safari, links and plain
text all land in the same shelf. Take one tile or grab the stack and take the
lot; whatever the destination accepts leaves the shelf, and a refused drop
springs back. Pin a tile and it stays after a successful drag, ready for
the next destination.

**It hands out copies.** perch stages its own copy of whatever you drop and
hands out copies of that. It never moves, renames, edits or deletes the thing
you dragged in, so an interrupted drag cannot lose data. A tile only appears
once its copy has finished, so nothing on the shelf is half-written and quitting
doesn't lose it.

## What it doesn't do

- **No permissions.** No Accessibility, no Input Monitoring, no Screen
  Recording, no Dock icon.
- **No telemetry.** Nothing about your files is ever written to a log.
- **Nearly offline.** The only thing perch sends to the internet is an hourly
  look at its own release tag, and Settings turns that off. It also listens for
  an iPhone you paired yourself, encrypted end to end, over your network or
  peer-to-peer if there isn't one, and off in Settings if you don't want it.

## Made for agents

The shelf has a command line, and the command line means **coding agents can
hand you things**. `perch add <file>` puts anything on the shelf from any
script or terminal, so an agent that just rendered a chart, exported a report
or built an installer can leave it where your hand already is, instead of
naming a path in a wall of text for you to copy out.

perch ships an **agent skill**:
[`ai/SKILL.md`](https://github.com/hausfold/perch/blob/main/ai/SKILL.md) in the
repo, teaching Claude Code, Codex or any skill-reading agent when to
reach for the shelf, and just as deliberately what perch *won't* do for it
(nothing can list or clear your shelf from a script; that stays yours). Every
hausfold app ships a skill, written to one
[family standard](https://github.com/hausfold/workshop/blob/main/docs/agent-surface.md).

## Questions

**Where do my files live?** Where they already were. perch keeps its copies
inside its own sandbox container, and it only ever knows about the things you
handed it. The [privacy policy](/perch/privacy) is the whole story, and
it's shorter than this page.

**What licence?** [MIT](https://github.com/hausfold/perch/blob/main/LICENSE).
Every line of perch is public and readable on GitHub, and you may read it,
change it, build it and ship it. There is no paid tier, no licence key and no
feature held back: the app in these pages is the whole app.

**Another shelf app?** There are others, and they're good. perch's angle is
dependability and restraint: copies rather than moves, no permissions to grant,
no telemetry, and a shelf that lives where your hand already is.

## Inside a haus machine

perch runs perfectly well on its own, and that is how most people meet it. It is
also a **room** in [haus](/docs/haus), the macOS layer, where it is installed
and kept current with the rest of the machine, and where it can hand its
light/dark polarity to macOS. Standalone and in-the-house are the same app; only
the wiring differs. See [the Shelf room](/docs/haus/rooms/shelf).
