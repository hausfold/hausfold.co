perch is two halves: the shelf on your Mac, and a free companion app that
throws things at it from your phone. Do the Mac first — the phone pairs *to*
it.

You need **macOS 14** or newer, and **iOS 18** or newer for the phone half. A
notch is nice, not required: the shelf works on any Mac and any display, and
sits at the top middle of the screen where there's no camera housing. Nothing
here needs a terminal.

## 1. Turn one macOS setting off

Do this before you install anything, or your very first drag will vanish and
perch will look broken by the only gesture it has.

In **System Settings ▸ Desktop & Dock**, scroll to **Mission Control** and turn
*off* **"Drag windows to top of screen to enter Mission Control"**.

macOS arms that top-edge trigger for the whole of any drag — files included,
despite the setting's name — and it fires over the same band the shelf lives
in. Leave it on and the Dock takes your drop into Mission Control before perch
ever sees it. It's the only thing perch asks of your Mac, it's reversible in
the same place, and a [haus](/docs/haus) machine flips it for you.

## 2. Get perch

The [**latest release**](https://hausfold.co/download/perch) is a signed,
notarized download: unzip it and drag `Perch.app` into your **Applications**
folder. Gatekeeper clears it, so you get macOS's ordinary *"downloaded from the
Internet, are you sure?"* confirmation the first time you open it and nothing
else — no *unidentified developer* block, no right-click-Open, no `xattr`.

Or, if you have Homebrew:

```sh
brew install --cask hausfold/tap/perch
```

Same build either way. No account, no sign-in, no licence key.

**There is no Dock icon** — that's deliberate. After you open it, look for a
small tray icon in your menu bar, top right. That's perch, and it's how you
reach Settings.

## 3. Turn on Launch at Login

Menu bar tray icon ▸ **Settings…** ▸ **General** ▸ **Launch at Login**.

Without it perch is gone after your next restart, which is an odd way to find
out you liked it. It comes back in the menu bar only: no window, no Dock icon.

## 4. Try it

Grab any file and start dragging it *up toward the notch*. A shelf drops down
to catch it. Let go.

Pile in as much as you like, from as many places as you like, then drag a tile
back out to wherever it's actually going. Your originals never move — perch
copies in and copies out. [Using the shelf](/docs/perch/using) is what to read
next.

## 5. The phone half

[**Perch Companion**](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6799443735) is free on the
App Store, for iPhone and iPad.

To pair it, once:

1. On the **Mac**: menu bar tray icon ▸ **Settings…** ▸ **Devices** ▸
   **Pair a Device…**, which shows a QR code.
2. On the **phone**: open Perch Companion and scan it.

After that, share anything from the phone — photos, files, links, text — and it
lands on the Mac's shelf. It goes over your own network, or straight
phone-to-Mac with no network at all, over the same radio AirDrop uses. Nothing
passes through a server; there isn't one, and the link is encrypted end to end.
Settings ▸ Devices tears the listener down if you'd rather it weren't there.

<Callout title="Turning Wi-Fi off doesn't take the link down">
Control Center's Wi-Fi toggle deliberately leaves AWDL (the direct radio link)
up, which is why your phone still finds a Mac that looks offline. Airplane
Mode, or **System Settings ▸ Wi-Fi ▸ Off**, is what actually ends it; so does
Settings ▸ Devices in perch. All the link ever needs is the Wi-Fi radio on at
both ends: no router, and not the same one.
</Callout>

## Updating

perch checks its own release tag once an hour. When there's a newer one, the
open shelf grows a quiet strip along its bottom edge and the menu bar menu
grows a matching row; dismissing a version dismisses that version, and the
next release asks again.

The button knows how you installed perch and does the right thing for that
install. Three of the four are owned by something that already updates them, so
the button hands you the command: `brew upgrade --cask perch`, `haus update` or
`nix flake update perch`. Swapping the bundle under Homebrew or haus would only
be undone by their next run.

A copy you dragged in yourself has no such owner, and it gets **Update Now**:
one click downloads the release, checks it is a build of ours that Apple
notarized, replaces the app and reopens it, in about as long as the download
takes. Your shelf survives it, because staged items live in perch's own
container rather than in the app bundle. If anything fails, perch says so in the
strip and the button goes back to opening the release page. It never leaves you
without an app.

Settings ▸ Updates turns the check off altogether.

## The terminal half

Everything above is the whole app. These two are for people who want perch in a
script.

### `perch add`

The command line ships **inside** the app, at
`Perch.app/Contents/MacOS/perch-cli`, so it is signed and notarized with it and
can never drift from the shelf it talks to.

The cask and the Nix flake both put it on your PATH as `perch`, and on a haus
machine the Shelf room does. If you installed by dragging the app, link it
yourself, once:

```sh
ln -s /Applications/Perch.app/Contents/MacOS/perch-cli /usr/local/bin/perch
```

[Using the shelf](/docs/perch/using) has what it does with a path once it has
one.

### Nix

The flake wraps the same notarized build rather than compiling from source: add
`github:hausfold/perch` as an input and its `overlays.default` puts `perch` in
your pkgs.

## On a haus machine

If your Mac runs [haus](/docs/haus), let the Shelf room own perch instead; it
installs it, turns the Mission Control setting off for you, and keeps perch
current with everything else, surviving a rebuild:

```nix
haus.shelf.enable = true;
```

```sh
haus rebuild
```

If you already installed it with Homebrew, letting the room own it is the
upgrade. [The Shelf room](/docs/haus/rooms/shelf) has the one option it adds.
